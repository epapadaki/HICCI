Welcome to HICCI

From a single-cell dataset to curated cell–cell communication. The platform infers ligand–receptor interactions, documents the biology behind each one, checks it against your own data, and keeps the expert’s judgement at the centre.

Upload your own dataset

What the dataset must look like
  • One AnnData file (.h5ad): cells × genes, as written by scanpy or anndata.
  • Expression values: normalised, log-transformed values are expected. If the main matrix holds raw counts, keep a normalised layer and choose it when preparing the dataset.
  • Genes: human gene symbols (HGNC, for example CD74, TREM2) as gene names, or in a column of var.
  • Cell types: one column of obs with the annotated populations, or a separate TSV/CSV file with a cell identifier column and a cell-type column.
  • Optional: a sample or donor column and a condition column (for example disease and control), for comparisons between groups.
  • Size: at least 10 cells per population; very small populations give unreliable predictions with any tool.
How a tool is chosen for your data
  • Population sizes: CellChat’s statistical test drops populations with fewer than 10 cells; CellPhoneDB keeps them.
  • Dataset size: with many cells or populations, faster tools (CellPhoneDB, NATMI) are preferred to CellChat’s permutation test.
  • Multi-subunit receptors: CellPhoneDB, CellChat and ICELLNET keep complexes (integrins, cytokine receptors) whole; NATMI and SingleCellSignalR work on single genes.
  • Statistics and pathways: CellChat gives a p-value and a signalling pathway for each interaction.
  • Your question: for immune signalling (cytokines, chemokines, checkpoints), ICELLNET’s strictly curated database is a strong choice.

No tool is best in general; tools and their databases disagree substantially. The recommendation is about suitability for your data.

Example: melanoma tumour microenvironment

        

        
A human melanoma tumour microenvironment dataset from Tirosh et al. 2016 (GEO GSE72056): malignant cells together with the main stromal and immune populations — T cells, B cells, NK cells, macrophages, endothelial cells and CAFs.

        
    
          
  • Explore predicted interactions such as TGFB1 → TGFBR2 (CAF → T cell) or CXCL12 → CXCR4 (CAF → malignant).
    • 
          
  • See the mechanism picture, the literature, the expression in the populations and the receiver’s response.
    • 
          
  • Record an assessment for each interaction and watch the curated network sharpen from a dense hairball to a compact, melanoma-specific signalling network.
    •
